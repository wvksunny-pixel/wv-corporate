HAPPYMOVE2026 VIETNAMFinal Report

A time to learn, share, and grow together

The Hyundai Motor Group Happy Move Global Youth Volunteers
spent their days learning alongside the children of Quyet Thang, Hai Phong, Vietnam,
building a better school and healthier everyday lives.

From school renovation to STEAM education, hygiene education and cultural exchange.
Small moves by young people grew into children’s learning and dreams,
and together, people from two countries discovered new possibilities.

9 days · July 26 – August 3, 2026

More about the STEAM Education Camp
Happy Move STEAM Education CampCamp leaders who design and run their own STEAM content

This participatory camp follows the STEAM education direction of Vietnam’s Ministry of Education. Going beyond conventional volunteering, Happy Move volunteers take part as camp leaders who design and run STEAM (science, technology, engineering, arts, math) content themselves.

What we built together

Korean and Vietnamese volunteers designed lesson plans and prepared materials together, running three hands-on sessions with 178 secondary students. Students worked in teams to build, test and present their STEAM products.

What changed for children

The joy of learning by doingBy experimenting and trying again, they applied classroom knowledge to real situations.
Room to thinkThey built creativity, critical thinking and confidence in testing ideas.
A reason to love scienceSolving problems together built teamwork and greater interest in math, science and engineering.

And what we learned

Sharing ideas and dividing roles, we learned to adapt lessons to students’ needs.

Meeting 178 students in person showed us the value of our work, and deepened our responsibility and empathy toward the community.

Education · Oral hygiene ACTIVITY 03

Healthy habits, every day

Oral hygiene session “Healthy Explorers” · Tu Cuong Preschool
275children received hygiene kits

Through play, children learned brushing and handwashing — healthy habits they can keep up every day.

Healthy Explorers lesson flow

Mission 1Find the cavity monster — why cavities happen
Mission 2Strong brushing — 3 times a day, within 3 minutes of eating, for 3 minutes
Mission 3Sparkle practice — toothbrushing in small groups
Wrap-upOral care kits · group photo

What we built together

After the volunteers’ performances and demonstrations, children practiced the steps of toothbrushing and handwashing in class. 275 children received individual hygiene kits.

What changed for children

Self-care habitsThey learned correct toothbrushing and handwashing by doing it.
Health made funLively performances built positive attitudes toward healthy routines and self-care.
Habits that continue at homeWith teachers and parents involved and kits in hand, children can keep these habits at school and at home.

And what we learned

We learned to turn technical hygiene steps into age-appropriate stories, dances and hands-on activities.

Community giving · Bicycles ACTIVITY 05

Bringing school a little closer

Bicycle distribution · with Quyet Thang People’s Committee
20bikesfor 20 students from disadvantaged families

Children from the most disadvantaged families now have bicycles for school. Less time on the road is expected to mean more time to learn and rest.

What we built together

Quyet Thang People’s Committee selected 20 students from the most disadvantaged families, and Hyundai Motor Group and World Vision presented 20 bicycles for their school travel.

More time to learnShorter travel leaves more time for study, homework and extracurricular activities.
Enough restBetter rest means better focus in class.
Staying in schoolBicycles are expected to help reduce dropout and support secondary school enrollment.

And the trust we built

With the local government selecting students and joining the handover, the partnership showed respect for local administrative structures.

This trust can lay a foundation for longer-term collaboration.

Lessons & next steps

Lessons and Next Steps

What worked well

  1. Stable operations through local partnershipHyundai Motor Group, World Vision Korea, World Vision Vietnam, the schools and the Quyet Thang People’s Committee shared roles, so the planned education, renovation and cultural exchange activities went ahead as planned.
  2. Hands-on work combining education and renovationImproving school spaces while running STEAM and hygiene lessons supported both the places where children live and learn and the learning itself.
  3. Korean and Vietnamese students working togetherVietnamese university volunteers joined the lessons and cultural exchange alongside Korean students, laying a basis for cooperation with local youth.

Challenges

  1. Adjusting renovation work for rainFrequent rain limited outdoor renovation, so the order and timing of work were adjusted on site.
  2. Coordination in a new areaAs Happy Move’s first program in this area, roles, schedules and site operations had to be aligned with local partners.

Recommendations for the next program

  1. Earlier, more detailed coordinationBefore deployment, agree schedules, roles and the scope of facility work in more detail with schools, local government and World Vision Vietnam.
  2. Schedules that allow for local conditionsBuild in spare time for renovation work, given possible changes from rain and other local conditions.
  3. Activities designed around local needsConfirm the real needs of schools and the community in advance and size the education and facility work to fit the site.
